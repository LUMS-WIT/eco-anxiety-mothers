Before you start

  • This is a research prototype based on interviews with 112 mothers in Rawalpindi in September and October 2026.
  • It gives likelihoods for groups of similar mothers. It cannot diagnose anyone and should not be used to screen real people.
  • The research findings are associations from other places. They point to possible effects but do not prove them.
  • If you or someone you know is struggling, please talk to a doctor or a mental health professional.

    How the agent learned

    Each of its 5 versions learned from different interviews, in a loop: answer as some surveyed mothers, compare with their real answers, propose a claim about what matters, check the claim on the interviews with code, and keep a lesson only if it is supported and it improves answers for other mothers. Press replay to watch a version's real history.

    Which AI does what

    • Answers, likelihoods and explanations: a local AI model (Qwen 3.5 9B) on our laptop, recorded on 16 Nov 2026 for the example mothers on this page.
    • Lessons: learned from the interviews and checked by code before being kept.
    • Research evidence: PubMed studies, screened once beforehand by a decision model (Jev).

    How good is it?

    Method and limits, in more detail

    Research: PubMed was searched for each condition three ways (systematic reviews; studies of women or mothers; South Asia and low- and middle-income countries). Each abstract was screened: is it about people, does it measure the condition and depression, anxiety or distress, and in which direction. In a blind check of 30 studies this screening agreed 80% of the time (100% on clear-cut ones). A likelihood comes from the share of relevant studies finding more distress; fewer than 3 studies means "too little evidence to say".

    The agent: the local model reads her profile and its instructions (short research notes and lessons it checked on real interviews) and gives a probability for each answer to the 15 questions of two standard questionnaires (PHQ-8 for depression, GAD-7 for anxiety). A score of 10 or more is the usual "likely" threshold. Five versions, each trained on different interviews, are combined.

    Limits: 112 mothers in one district over five weeks; most studies come from other countries; abstracts can be misread. The likelihoods describe groups of mothers and the research. They cannot predict one woman's future. This is a prototype for discussion.

    About the data

    Privacy

    • Only group results are shown here; no individual woman's answers.
    • The agent learned from interviews on our laptop; its lessons contain only group numbers.
    • The mothers on this page are invented examples. Nothing you click is sent anywhere.

    What the numbers can and cannot tell

    • They show associations in one district over about five weeks.
    • Scores also fell over the fieldwork weeks; heat and season may play a part.
    • The agent ranks risk reasonably for depression, less well for anxiety, and it is not more accurate than simple statistics for any one mother.
